About

My UI/UX journey started in high school Physics II.

Physics explains the world through math, which asks for intuition in a lot of places at once. Most classes lead with the equations, but I noticed my friends understood things faster when they could see them.

How it started

That kicked off my passion for interactive products that help people do things more easily.

So I started making demos. In Microsoft PowerPoint, I animated simple, interactive slides about electromagnetism, with instructions to guide you through each step. They were only for a couple of friends, but I loved watching them enjoy the demos and understand the physics more intuitively than a formula sheet ever let them.

Since then I've worked across creative technology, UI/UX design, human-computer interaction and art, chasing the same fun I had in PowerPoint.

Along the way I've grown more interested in accessibility and usability: how to make technology enjoyable and usable by everyone, without discrimination.

UI/UX is a modern School of Athens.

In Raphael's fresco, thinkers from every field share one hall. UI/UX works the same way. It brings together people with different backgrounds, curiosities and passions, and treats those differences as new perspectives that make an experience richer.

That's where my interdisciplinary background becomes a strength. Knowing how different fields approach a problem shapes my ideas, and makes me eager to try new approaches.
